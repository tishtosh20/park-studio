THE PARK THAT WAS

STUYVESANT COVE PARK / NYC
FIELD NOTES / PRESENT DAY

Google Maps hasn't quite caught up with Stuyvesant Cove Park.

The Street View imagery still shows an older version of the waterfront, before much of it was redesigned. I've spent a lot of time here, so walking through this version feels strange. It's familiar, but almost everything is slightly out of place. More greenery and more seating.

These are a few things I've noticed while comparing it to the park today.

The entrance is one of the biggest changes. The old green spaces and benches are gone, and flood walls now separate parts of the park from the path behind it. People still pass through constantly, especially runners using the bike path, but the way the space is organized feels completely different. It feels more like a proper distinction between the park and the path and feels much more industrial. This is due to Hurricane Sandy, which caused a lot of damage to the waterfront and led to the construction of flood walls and other protective measures.

What used to be a greener stretch of the park now has amphitheatre-like seating that extends much further back. It's a pretty significant change in how the space is used. Instead of just walking past the greenery, people now have somewhere to sit, gather, and look out at the water.

This is a smaller change, but one I keep thinking about. The railings are wooden now, which I only noticed recently when I saw rainwater collecting on them. I'm not sure exactly when they were replaced, but it's funny how something you walk past all the time can suddenly look completely new.

There's still plenty of greenery, but it occupies the park differently now. Some of the planted areas visible in Street View have been replaced or rearranged to make room for paths and seating. It's interesting to see how much the layout has changed without the park necessarily feeling any less green.
